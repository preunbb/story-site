# Story structural review

Structural review is **not** copy-editing. Do not fix typos or grammar here unless they block understanding. For spelling/grammar, use the `story-copy-edit` skill instead.

## Scope

Audit for problems a reader would notice:

- **Continuity** — character facts, injuries, castration/orchiectomy state, wardrobe, location, time of day, who is present
- **Action clarity** — who did what to whom, in what order, from what position; blocking that cannot be staged
- **Timeline** — impossible travel, events out of order, elapsed time that does not add up
- **POV** — head-hopping without signal, knowledge a POV character should not have
- **Repetition** — unintentional (see below); distinguish from deliberate rhetorical echo
- **Deception & misdirection** — character lies, half-truths, and unreliable on-page claims (see below); **not** plot holes unless the *narrative* contradicts itself
- **Cross-story canon** — when the story shares cast with the catalog (see `data/characters.js`)

## How to read the story

1. Resolve the story in `data/stories.js` (id, title, cast).
2. Read prose from **one** source:
   - **Preferred for full text:** synced `assets/stories/<id>.md` (read-only)
   - **Or** Google Doc via `dist/drive_doc_ids.json` + Google Workspace MCP
3. For long stories, review **chapter by chapter**; note chapter headings while scanning.
4. Load `data/characters.js` for any named character; same first name across the catalog = same person.

**Never edit `assets/stories/*.md`** — it is regenerated by sync. Report issues; propose Drive doc fixes only if the user asks you to implement.

## Review checklist

Work through these in order. Skip categories that clearly do not apply.

```
Progress:
- [ ] Cast & state ledger built
- [ ] Timeline mapped (if non-trivial)
- [ ] Deception ledger triaged (lies vs contradictions)
- [ ] Action sequences staged
- [ ] Repetition triaged
- [ ] Cross-story continuity checked (if applicable)
- [ ] Report written
```

### 1. Cast & state ledger

While reading, track per character:

- Physical state (especially genital injury/castration status when relevant)
- Location and who is in the scene
- Knowledge (what they have seen/heard)
- Names, nicknames, relationships

Flag when prose contradicts an earlier beat **in the same story** or established catalog canon.

**Do not flag** when only a *character's spoken claim* contradicts canon if the story elsewhere supports deliberate deception (see §2a).

### 2a. Deception, half-truths, and misdirection

Many catalog scenes hinge on groomers, cult rites, or dommes **lying to victims**. Before filing a continuity finding, ask:

1. **Who is speaking?** Victim-facing dialogue is often false by design.
2. **Does the narrative know?** Look for winks: internal monologue, sibling asides, later reveal, or lore the reader already has (e.g. Ch.14 rite definitions vs Ch.18 Abby's "you won't get a popped testicle").
3. **Is there dramatic irony?** The reader is meant to understand the gap (Elijah promised "no half-man" while invited as a Second Rite Offering).
4. **Does ground truth stay consistent?** Track **actual** outcomes in the cast ledger, not what characters were told.

| Pattern | Usually intentional misdirection | Usually a real plot hole |
|---------|----------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Cultist promises victim safety | Yes — bait for rite | — |
| Victim recall vs what we saw | Yes — unreliable memory or parsed lie | Victim recall matches a scene we never got |
| Speaker A tells victim X; speaker B tells victim Y | Yes — different grooming scripts | Same speaker gives incompatible **facts to the audience** in close succession without motive |
| Terminology shift ("First Rite" in dialogue) | Yes — weaponized ambiguity | Glossary chapter defines term one way; **narrator** uses it the opposite way with no irony |
| Injury state | — | Prose/narrator says left nut gone; next scene narrator treats it as intact |

**Workflow:** When a contradiction appears, tag it **Deception (confirmed)**, **Deception (likely)**, or **Contradiction (hole)** before assigning severity. Only **Contradiction (hole)** belongs in Critical/Major unless the deceptive beat itself is unclear to readers (then **Ambiguous**).

Evidence quotes should name **speaker** and **audience** ("Abby to Elijah backstage" vs "narrator re Isaac's sack").

### 2. Timeline

Note time jumps, flashbacks, and "later that evening" transitions. Flag:

- Events that require more time than allowed
- A character in two places without explanation
- Consequences appearing before causes

### 3. Action sequences

For fight scenes, sex scenes, medical procedures, and multi-person blocking:

- Draw a minimal mental stage: positions, facing, who is restrained/free
- Step through beats in order; one action per beat
- Flag when a limb/body part is in two places, an attacker could not reach the target, someone reacts before the cause, or pronouns/antecedents are ambiguous

Quote the **shortest passage** that breaks staging; do not rewrite unless asked.

### 4. Repetition triage

| Signal | Likely intentional | Likely unintentional |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Same word/phrase | Anaphora, mantra, ritual liturgy, comedic rule-of-three | Same unusual verb twice in one paragraph |
| Same beat | Emphasis ("again," "once more," escalating ritual) | Two paragraphs describe the same action without new detail |
| Same explanation | Theme, callback to earlier chapter | Re-introducing a fact the reader already knows |

When unsure, label **Ambiguous** and explain both readings.

### 5. Cross-story continuity

When the story uses catalog characters (Melody, Cathy, etc.):

- Compare age, role, and prior injuries against other stories only when this story **implies** shared history
- Do not demand retcons across unrelated tales; flag only **direct contradictions** the reader would catch

## Severity

- **Critical** — impossible to reconcile; breaks plot or staging
- **Major** — reader will pause or reread; likely confusion
- **Minor** — polish; clarity tweak would help
- **Ambiguous** — may be intentional; author should decide

## Rules of engagement

- **Read before judging** — cite line-adjacent quotes or chapter/paragraph anchors.
- **Do not rewrite voice** — diagnose; let the author fix.
- **Proportional depth** — short story: one pass; novel-length: per-chapter notes then rollup.
- **Separate skills** — typos → `story-copy-edit`; scene images → `story-image-iterate`.
